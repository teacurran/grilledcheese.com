A new federated social network

One account.
Both networks.

Grilled Cheese speaks ATProto and ActivityPub at once. Post once and Bluesky and Mastodon both get it. Follow anyone, on either network, without leaving.

Built new, front to back, to be fast, and to be the best place to post and read short-form content and media.

Use it in your browser Get the app

Most social apps are one network. Grilled Cheese is on two at once.

It speaks ATProto, the protocol Bluesky runs on, and ActivityPub, the protocol Mastodon and the rest of the fediverse run on. You post once and both networks get it. You follow one account and it does not matter which network that account lives on. Nobody has to sign up here to read you, and you do not have to leave to read them.

Search for someone by their Bluesky handle or their fediverse address and follow them. Their posts land in your Following feed alongside everyone else, and their follows, likes and replies reach you the same way.

NetworkWhat people type to find you
ATProto (Bluesky and friends)@you.grilledcheese.app
ActivityPub (Mastodon and friends)@you@grilledcheese.app

The badge under a profile name says which networks that identity is reachable on. The two networks are not the same shape, so a post does not always arrive looking the way you wrote it. What crosses, and what it looks like when it arrives is written down in full.

Five identities. One login.

Your account is you: one email address, one password, one set of security settings. Your identities are the presences you keep, each with its own handle, profile, followers, posts and preferences. Free accounts include five, and none of them needs a separate email address.

They are not sub-accounts and not aliases. To everyone else, two of your identities look like two unrelated people. Nothing on a public profile connects them, and the app never posts as one while you think you are the other. The switcher is a button in the menu, and choosing an identity reloads the whole app as that persona: its feed, its notifications, its settings, its drafts.

Belongs to the account

  • Email address and password
  • Two-factor authentication and recovery codes
  • Sessions and remembered devices
  • Premium subscription

Belongs to each identity

  • Handle, display name, avatar, banner, bio
  • Followers and following
  • Posts, replies, bookmarks
  • Privacy, notification and content preferences
  • Federation settings

The split is what lets one identity be private while another is public, and what makes a change to your password protect all of them at once.

Pick the domain, not just the name

A new identity asks for two things: a username, and the domain it lives on. Grilled Cheese serves several domains, and the handle is the two together.

Or bring your own domain

If you own a domain, you can use it as your handle, so @you.example.com replaces @you.grilledcheese.app. That is how handles work on the wider ATProto network: the domain is the name, and all Grilled Cheese does is check that the domain agrees with you about who it belongs to. Because the domain is yours, the handle is yours too. Your name does not depend on us continuing to exist, and it comes with you if you ever move your identity to another provider, which a grilledcheese.app handle cannot.

The cost is one DNS record. Settings → Handle shows the two records you can publish to prove the domain is yours, either of which is enough, and they have to be published before you submit, because the server checks the moment you do. Your fediverse address does not change: to Mastodon you stay @you@grilledcheese.app, whatever your ATProto handle is.

Organizations on their own domain

An organization can point a domain it owns at Grilled Cheese. Once the domain is validated, the organization can grant identities on it, you@yourcompany.com, to member accounts. Those work exactly like any other identity: the member switches to it, posts from it, and keeps it separate from their personal presences.

Home is not one timeline.

It is one feed you look at through a filter, and the filter is yours to change at any moment. Every identity has two sources: Following, the accounts it follows wherever they live, and Discovery, posts from across both networks that Grilled Cheese thinks you might want. If you follow nobody yet, Home shows you Discovery. A new account is not an empty room.

The switch at the top of Home chooses what kind of thing the feed shows you:

Photos
Photo posts only, edge to edge, one square image per cell.
Videos
Video posts only, playing full-bleed as you scroll, from Grilled Cheese and from the networks it federates with.
Text
Text posts only. The Twitter-shaped face.
Everything
No filter at all.

The compose button changes with the face you are on: a camera in Photos, a camcorder in Videos, a pencil in Text. Whatever you are looking at, the button makes more of it.

Conversations are end-to-end encrypted.

Private messages between Grilled Cheese accounts use the Messaging Layer Security standard, RFC 9420. Grilled Cheese's servers carry the messages but cannot read them. A conversation can be one-to-one or a group, and it belongs to the identity you are using, like everything else.

Someone on Mastodon can message you too, and it arrives as a conversation here. ActivityPub has no message encryption, so that thread is not encrypted, and the app marks it plainly rather than let the lock icon claim something untrue.

Post the way you meant to.

The composer takes rich text and, with it, up to ten photos or one video. Beyond that you can:

  • Add a poll.
  • Choose who can see it, and who can reply.
  • Declare the language it is written in, so it reaches the right readers.
  • Schedule it for later rather than posting now.
  • Put a content warning on it, so it stays covered until a reader chooses to open it.
  • Describe every photo. The alt text reminder is on by default, and an identity can be set to require it.

And the rest of it.

  • Works without a connection

    Grilled Cheese keeps the last feed it loaded. Open the app offline and you get that timeline with a banner saying so, not an error page. Anything you post waits in an outbox and goes out when the connection comes back.

  • Nine themes, light and dark

    Each built-in theme family has a light and a dark variant, and the app follows whichever your device is set to. Premium adds an editor for making your own, with the contrast of every pairing checked as you go.

  • Accessibility settings that stack

    Text size, higher contrast and reduced motion, per device. Text size is a multiplier over the system setting rather than a replacement for it.

  • Private identities

    On a private identity, follows arrive as requests you approve and posts are hidden from anyone who is not an approved follower. The app tells you exactly how each network treats that before you switch it on.

  • Your identity is yours to move

    Every identity is a full citizen of the AT Protocol network with its own permanent name, a DID. You can move it to another provider, or bring one here, and your followers, posts and likes come with it.

  • Connect AI tools

    Grilled Cheese speaks the Model Context Protocol. Mint a key and Claude, ChatGPT or any MCP client can read your timeline; with Premium it can post, follow and react as you. How connecting works.

  • Premium raises limits, and that is all

    Everything in Grilled Cheese works without it. Premium raises the scheduled-post and video-length limits and unlocks the theme editor. There is no Premium badge, and no priority in anyone's feed.

  • Moderation you can see

    Content warnings, hidden words, mute and block on your side; on the other, a moderation record that shows every action taken on your account, the guideline it cited, and its appeal.
